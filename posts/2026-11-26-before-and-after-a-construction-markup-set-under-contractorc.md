# Before and After: A Construction Markup Set Under ContractorCounter's Single Name Field

Release notes tell you what changed. Walkthroughs show you what it feels like. This page takes a recent change to construction markup in [ContractorCounter](https://www.contractorcounter.com) and walks through a hypothetical drawing review twice: once with the old two-field approach and once with the new one.

The change itself: markups now have a single Name instead of separate Subject and Name fields, and the Markup List shows and groups markups by that Name.

> The project, people and numbers below are an illustrative example, not real data.

## The setup

Picture a small tenant improvement. Three people mark up the same set of floor plans and ceiling plans over two days:

- **Ana**, the estimator, flags items that affect the price.
- **Ben**, the project manager, flags questions for the owner and architect.
- **Cy**, an assistant estimator, helps on the second day.

Between them, they place about sixty markups.

## Before: two fields, three habits

With both Subject and Name available, each person develops a habit.

- Ana puts the trade in Subject ("Doors") and the issue in Name ("Hardware set missing").
- Ben puts the issue in Subject ("Owner question") and mostly leaves Name blank.
- Cy, new to the team, puts everything in Name and skips Subject.

Every habit is reasonable. Together, they cause problems.

### What the review looks like

On day three, Ben wants to know how many owner questions are open. Some are in Subject. Some, from Cy, are in Name. Some of Ana's are owner questions too, but they are filed under a trade. Answering the question means reading every markup.

Ana wants to price all the door hardware issues. Hers are easy to find. Cy's are worded differently and live in a different field.

Nobody did anything wrong. The data just does not line up.

## After: one Name, one list

Now replay the same project with a single Name per markup. Before starting, the three agree on a short list:

- Owner Question
- Door Hardware
- Missing Dimension
- Ceiling Conflict
- Verify In Field

Each person places markups and gives each one a Name from the list.

### What the review looks like

Ben opens the Markup List. Because it groups by Name, the owner questions are already together under "Owner Question." He reads that group, not the whole list.

Ana opens the "Door Hardware" group. All of the door hardware markups, from all three people, are in one place.

On day two, Cy types "Door Hardwear" on two markups. In the Markup List, those two appear as their own small group next to the main "Door Hardware" group. Ana notices quickly and fixes them.

## What actually changed for the team

| Situation | Two fields | One Name |
|---|---|---|
| Deciding how to label a markup | Two choices, plus how they relate | One choice |
| Finding all items of one kind | Search across fields and habits | Open one group |
| Onboarding a new teammate | Explain a two-field convention | Hand over a list of Names |
| Catching inconsistent labels | Hard to see in a flat list | Shows as a separate group |

## What did not change

The drawings did not change. Markups are still placed where the issues are, and people still choose their own words. The update does not force a vocabulary on anyone.

What changed is that there is one place for those words, the list shows them, and grouping by them makes agreement and disagreement equally visible.

## Lessons from the example

1. **Agree on Names before the session.** Five minutes of agreement saved Ben and Ana a lot of reading.
2. **Name the kind of issue.** Every Name on the list could be shared by many markups.
3. **Use the grouped list as a check.** Cy's typo was caught because it formed its own group.
4. **Keep the list short.** Five Names covered sixty markups.

## Try it

If your team marks up shared drawings, try this on your next project: agree on a few Names, mark up as usual, then open the Markup List and see how the groups line up.
