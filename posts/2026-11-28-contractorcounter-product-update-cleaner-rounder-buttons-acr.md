# ContractorCounter product update: cleaner, rounder buttons

This page covers the latest visual update to [ContractorCounter](https://www.contractorcounter.com), the construction takeoff and estimating software built for subcontractors and small trade teams. If you're new here, there's a short introduction to the product further down.

## At a glance

- **What changed:** buttons across ContractorCounter now have a cleaner, rounder look
- **Where:** across the product
- **Type:** visual update
- **What you need to know:** you'll see the new look next time you use ContractorCounter

## The update in detail

Buttons throughout ContractorCounter have been restyled. They are cleaner and rounder, and the new style is used across the product rather than on a single screen.

That's the full extent of what this update covers. It isn't being presented as a new feature, a change to how construction takeoff works, or a change to pricing. Keeping the description that precise is deliberate: if you use a tool to price real work, you deserve to know exactly what changed and what didn't get announced.

## Why small details get attention

In estimating software, the content on screen is demanding. Construction drawings carry a lot of detail, and reading them carefully is the core of good takeoff. The interface around them should support that reading, not compete with it.

Buttons are the controls you use at every stage, on every job. That makes them one of the few parts of the product where a small improvement reaches everyone, every time. Cleaner, rounder and consistent across the product, they're meant to keep the workspace calm while you focus on the drawings and the numbers.

## New to ContractorCounter?

ContractorCounter is construction takeoff and estimating software that runs in a single browser workspace. In one place, you can:

1. take off construction drawings
2. review AI-generated counts
3. add your rates
4. build quotes

The product covers construction takeoff, drawing markup and estimating. Because everything happens in one workspace, you don't need to move the job between separate programs as it goes from drawing to quote.

## Who it's built for

ContractorCounter is built for subcontractors and small trade teams. That shapes decisions like this update. In a small business, estimating usually happens alongside site work, ordering and admin, often in short sessions spread through the week. A tidy, consistent interface is easier to come back to when you've been away from the screen for a few days.

## Tips for teams after a visual update

Even small visual changes can make someone pause. A few simple steps help:

- **Give everyone a heads-up.** One line in your team chat is enough.
- **Include occasional users.** Owners and office staff who only open the tool now and then are the most likely to notice something looks different.
- **Look around on an old job first.** It's easier to get used to any change when there's no deadline attached.
- **Know where help lives.** Bookmark the help centre and guides so questions don't have to wait.
